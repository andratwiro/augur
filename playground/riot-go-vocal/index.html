What RIOT is in one breath

It makes political representation falsifiable.

It takes the real decisions a body makes — a council, a parliament — and turns each one into a plain-language card a 12-year-old can vote.

  • Vote it blind. For / abstain / against — before any party position or outcome is shown. No anchoring.
  • Then reveal. How each party actually voted, always linked back to the source minutes.
  • See the distance. Your % affinity with each party, and where you sit on the map.

Representation stops being a vibe and becomes a measurable hit-rate.

Ruling out the obvious move

Why it isn't another phase method.

The reflex is to make RIOT the 9th method — a phase you configure. It doesn't fit, three ways:

  • It's a different object. A phase aggregates input. RIOT surfaces decisions. Forcing the outputs of power into one-method-per-phase misrepresents what it is.
  • It's cross-project by nature. RIOT spans a body's whole record, over time — you look back and forth across it. A project is one time-bounded engagement; this is a standing surface.
  • It's automated, not configured. Staff don't author RIOT cards. The pipeline does, as decisions land. Configuration is one connection, not a setup form.

The GHOST, honestly

Triage, not representation.

RIOT's boldest piece is the GHOST — an AI proxy that votes your deck blind and predicts you. I would not sell it as representation. No AI represents you, and the proof today rests on one ballot.

Sell it as triage. Nobody can follow every body that governs them. The GHOST is a copilot you can MCP into the council's record:

“Where is this? What changed? What would I likely think?” — it reads the minutes and hands you a reasoned lean. The vote stays yours.

That framing dodges the legitimacy minefield — and it's the more honest product anyway.

The rules that can't bend

Drop one and it keeps the UI, loses the point.

Blind voting

You decide before you see parties or outcome.

Contested-filter

Only score genuinely divisive decisions — unanimous votes inflate everyone.

Source-traceable

Every card links to the minutes. No exceptions.

Same neutral facts

Human and AI read the same dossier; opinion lives only in the human layers.

Privacy by construction

The values profile never leaves the resident.

Non-partisan framing

Name systemic forces, never sides.

For GV these aren't RIOT's quirks — they're what keeps a city's trust intact.

The honest hard parts

What I'd want us clear-eyed about.

  • Pipeline cost per chamber. Ingesting each council's records is real engineering plus human curation. Only Reus & Brussels are real today. This scales per-city, not per-tenant.
  • Outputs-of-power is new turf. GV's whole muscle is collecting input, not surfacing decisions. This asks the org to do something genuinely adjacent to its core.
  • GHOST discipline. The triage framing only holds if we hold it. The moment it's pitched as “AI represents you,” both the legitimacy and the one-ballot proof wobble.

Where I'd start

The smallest real test half-exists.

  1. Pick the chamber that's already real. Brussels has a live pipeline; Copenhagen runs on GV. The two ends almost meet.
  2. Ship the townhall module. One chamber, GV identity for humanity, contested-filter on, the GHOST shipped triage-only.
  3. Answer the gating question first: which GV cities have machine-readable council records, and can a GSM run the curation loop?
  4. Then watch for habit. Is “five minutes at the townhall” something a city will pay to keep?

In one line

Not a feature. A new altitude.

Go Vocal gathers what citizens want. RIOT shows what power did — and lets them check the distance.

The bet is that closing that loop is worth five minutes a week.
