The insight

Our participation rates aren't a marketing problem. Most residents who arrive never reach the point of contributing — and most staff who launch a project never see whether they could. We've been auditing how the product is built, not whether anyone can finish using it.

The product is one loop, not two backlogs.A project residents can't finish is a failed project — and a low completion rate becomes thin, unrepresentative analysis for the city.
The friction lives in the seams.Between the two surfaces — exactly the gap a surface-by-surface audit is built to miss.

Who we audit for · sampling & recruitment

Five people, two audiences, two recruiting funnels — never one pooled "5."

Back office — Joan (power user, slowed by hidden state) and Diane (low savviness, stranded by the empty-shell create flow). Front office, on a phone — the rare participant, engaged resident and skeptic. Two separate research streams — different pools, effort, incentives; each segment gets its own n; over-recruit 20–30% for no-shows.

Back office · staff (B2B)

  • Moderated-heavy, small qualified pool — we sit with them to probe the why. Qual: 5 / segment (Joan, Diane), counted separately.
  • Benchmark = within-subjects pre/post, so n is feasible: target 20, floor 15. Pairing is what buys the power with scarce admins.
  • Screen on tasks, not titles — projects run, methods configured. 2–4 wk lead time.

Front office · residents

  • Benchmark = between-subjects A/B, so it needs ~2× the BO n: target 40 / arm for usable CIs (±15pp success, 5-pt SUS). Tree test 50+. Budget-limited → run 5–8/arm as formative and say so.
  • Hard-to-reach residents — seniors, low digital skill, non-native: panels under-represent them. Recruit via NGOs, libraries, community orgs; in-person + on-site support.
  • Higher, concrete incentives — cash, transit/childcare cover. Match the session to real tech comfort, not default Zoom.
Civic platforms fail the very people panels miss — 56% of EU adults have only basic digital skills. If our sample skews tech-confident, the audit certifies an experience the actual residents can't use.

Dimension 1 · accessibility · what binds us, what we test to

The cities owe WCAG 2.1 AA today. We build to 2.2 AA — that's the low-regret move.

Get the standard exactly right, because it's a procurement gate. The legally operative floor in the EU now is WCAG 2.1 AA, via the harmonised EN 301 549 v3.2.1. Auditing to 2.2 AA satisfies 2.1 AA, fixes the nine criteria new in 2.2 (target size, dragging, accessible auth, focus-not-obscured…), and front-runs EN 301 549 v4 / WCAG 2.2, expected to harmonise ~2026.

WAD
The binding path. Every public-body client must meet EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA and publish an accessibility statement. It flows to us through procurement — a city can't buy a non-conformant platform.

EAA
Convergent pressure. In force Jun 2025 for consumer services. Its reach over pure-B2B municipal SaaS is genuinely contested — we don't lean on it, but it raises the same EN 301 549 bar market-wide.

Dimension 1 · the checks we actually run

Automated catches a third. The criteria that bite are manual.

axe-core / Lighthouse / Pa11y sweep every template first — they reliably catch missing labels, contrast, ARIA misuse and autocomplete, but only ~30–40% of the SC. The rest — errors, focus management, target size, redundant entry — we test by hand on representative templates: a form, a multi-step flow, an interactive map, a content page.

Dimension 2 · AI we can trust

An AI summary becomes what the city believes residents said.

Sensemaking, Perspectives and FormSync turn raw input into the read officials act on — so the bar isn't "is it clever," it's calibrated trust: residents and staff should trust each result exactly as much as it has earned. We audit on four axes, then walk each feature against them on the next slide.

Trust

Provenance + honest confidence, coverage disclosed ("380 of 412 inputs"), data-use stated. No decorative percentages.

PAIR calibrated-trust

Explainability

Every claim, cluster and tag traces in one click to its source input, quote or image region.

MS HAX G11

Human override

All output editable in place; re-running warns before clobbering edits; nothing reaches residents without a human gate.

EU AI Act Art. 14 · HAX G9/G14

Representativeness

Minority & non-English views surfaced, not thresholded away — a bias measured at 34× random in LLM aggregation.

Stanford/MIT/Toronto 2025
For a civic tool the two highest-severity axes are human-override and representativeness: a failure there removes accountability or distorts democratic input. We treat any miss in those two as a blocker, not a nice-to-have.

The benchmark study · design

A baseline is only proof if the rule is set before we test.

A benchmark measures the redesign against today's product to show a delta. We pick the design per surface, lock 5–7 top-task scenarios with observable success states, and pre-register the ship rule — all before a single session.

The two designs we'll run

FO · A/BBetween-subjects. Today's UI vs redesign, different residents per arm. Tasks are short and repetitive (learning effects are real), and we can recruit volume. Cleanest causal claim.
BO · pre/postWithin-subjects. The same admin runs a config task on baseline then redesign, order counterbalanced. Admins are scarce; pairing buys the power.

The benchmarked tasks (locked wk 3)

  • FO: find & open a project · submit an input · budget/prioritise a vote · find when a phase closes
  • BO: create a project with a working phase + method · add a survey question · find the top input & change its status
  • Each gets a pre-registered success rubric + time-out (4 min FO / 6 min BO); wording never names a UI element.
The win condition, pre-registered: ship if redesign SUS ≥ baseline + 5 points AND no task's success rate regresses beyond its confidence interval. Set today, so the result can't be argued after the fact.

Between- vs within-subjects benchmark design · pre-registered success rubric + time-out · Sauro & Lewis (MeasuringU) · NN/g benchmarking.

The benchmark study · what we capture & how we read it

One instrument per task, one overall score, every number with a CI.

InstrumentMeasuresWhenRead it as
Task successEffectiveness — the headlinePer task, scored liveRate + adjusted-Wald 95% CI; 2-proportion (A/B) or McNemar (pre/post)
Time on taskEfficiencySuccessful attemptsSkewed → median + geometric mean, never plain mean
SEQ (1–7)Per-task difficultyRight after each taskPinpoints the hard task; Mann-Whitney / Wilcoxon
SUS (10-item)Overall usabilityEnd of session0–100 + percentile; t-test (A/B) or paired (pre/post)
Errors / assistsWhere it breaksPer taskModerator help = a failure in scoring

These benchmark numbers are the defensible ROI claim. Alongside them we pull the civic signals from product data — time-to-launch & ticket deflection (Intercom), FO completion & return rate, and representativeness vs population (who participates, not raw volume).

Reference bands for sanity, not targets: SUS 68 = average, aim 80+ · SEQ ≈ 5.5 · industry task-success ≈ 78%. We baseline our own values first. SEQ/SUS · MeasuringU / Sauro · Google HEART.

Pre-audit · the read

What this signals for the plan.

A two-day skim of the real product already validates the reframe — and sharpens where the six weeks should bite.

  • The seam is the story. The worst failures sit between surfaces — a created project with no method, a "we did" card linking to nothing. A surface-by-surface audit would miss exactly these; the service-blueprint lens is the right one.
  • Mobile front office is the priority, not a footnote. Cookie walls, postage-stamp maps, colliding sticky CTAs and a swipe-only Perspectives feed all degrade where most residents actually are — yet it's the half no prior pass watched.
  • Status & trust are the common thread (the spine of slide 6). Both surfaces hide their own state — "Mixed rights", "Unassigned", broken deltas, stale dates, 404s on real routes. Visibility-of-system-status is the heuristic to lead with.
  • Dead ends are cheap quick wins. 404s on valid routes, the citizenlab.co reply-to and the self-disclaiming audit log are concrete, low-effort fixes that buy trust on day one — proof the roadmap delivers early.

Preliminary, evidence-grounded read on the live product · Joan / Diane (BO) + resident personas (FO mobile). Not a substitute for the full audit.
