The reframe · and the one number it gives us

Most residents who arrive never reach the point of contributing — and most staff who launch a project never see whether they could. We've been auditing how the product is built, not whether anyone can finish using it.

So the audit moves one number: completed, representative participation per project.

Every recommendation has to ladder up to it — so priority is judged by how much it moves this, not by taste. The same number on both surfaces: a resident who finishes is a usable input for staff.

Who we audit for · sampling & recruitment

Five people, two audiences, two recruiting funnels — never one pooled "5."

Back office — Joan (power user, slowed by hidden state) and Diane (low savviness, stranded by the empty-shell create flow). Front office, on a phone — the rare participant, engaged resident and skeptic. Two separate research streams — different pools, effort, incentives; each segment gets its own n; over-recruit 20–30% for no-shows.

Back office · staff (B2B)

  • Moderated-heavy, small qualified pool — we sit with them to probe the why. Qual: 5 / segment (Joan, Diane), counted separately.
  • Benchmark = within-subjects pre/post, so n is feasible: target 20, floor 15. Pairing is what buys the power with scarce admins.
  • Screen on tasks, not titles — projects run, methods configured. 2–4 wk lead time.

Front office · residents

  • Benchmark = between-subjects A/B, so it needs ~2× the BO n: target 40 / arm for usable CIs (±15pp success, 5-pt SUS). Tree test 50+. Budget-limited → run 5–8/arm as formative and say so.
  • Hard-to-reach residents — seniors, low digital skill, non-native: panels under-represent them. Recruit via NGOs, libraries, community orgs; in-person + on-site support.
  • Higher, concrete incentives — cash, transit/childcare cover. Match the session to real tech comfort, not default Zoom.
Civic platforms fail the very people panels miss. If our sample skews tech-confident, the audit certifies an experience the actual residents can't use — and the north-star's representative half quietly fails.

Dimension 1 · accessibility · what binds us, what we test to

Cities owe WCAG 2.1 AA today. We audit to 2.2 AA.

It's a procurement gate, so get the standard exactly right. The legally operative floor in the EU now is WCAG 2.1 AA, via the harmonised EN 301 549 v3.2.1. Auditing to 2.2 AA satisfies 2.1 AA, fixes the nine criteria new in 2.2 (target size, dragging, accessible auth, focus-not-obscured…), and front-runs the next EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.2 harmonisation — the low-regret move.

WAD
The binding path. Every public-body client must meet EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA and publish an accessibility statement. It flows to us through procurement — a city can't buy a non-conformant platform.
Directive (EU) 2016/2102
EAA
Convergent pressure. In force Jun 2025 for consumer services. Its reach over pure-B2B municipal SaaS is genuinely contested — we don't lean on it, but it raises the same EN 301 549 bar market-wide.
Directive (EU) 2019/882
Output
A flagged-issue list mapped to SC + level — not a certification — plus real content for each city's own accessibility statement (conformance level, known gaps, feedback channel).
Caveat: confirm OJ-cited EN 301 549 version at audit time

Dimension 1 · the checks we actually run

Automated catches a third. The criteria that bite are manual.

axe-core / Lighthouse / Pa11y sweep every template first — they reliably catch missing labels, contrast, ARIA misuse and autocomplete, but only ~30–40% of the SC. The rest — errors, focus management, target size, redundant entry — we test by hand on representative templates: a form, a multi-step flow, an interactive map, a content page.

Dimension 2 · AI we can trust

An AI summary becomes what the city believes residents said.

Sensemaking, Perspectives and FormSync turn raw input into the read officials act on — so the bar isn't "is it clever," it's calibrated trust: residents and staff should trust each result exactly as much as it has earned. We audit on four axes, then walk each feature against them on the next slide.

Trust

Provenance + honest confidence, coverage disclosed ("380 of 412 inputs"), data-use stated. No decorative percentages.

PAIR calibrated-trust

Explainability

Every claim, cluster and tag traces in one click to its source input, quote or image region.

MS HAX G11

Human override

All output editable in place; re-running warns before clobbering edits; nothing reaches residents without a human gate.

EU AI Act Art. 14 · HAX G9/G14

Representativeness

Minority & non-English views surfaced, not thresholded away — LLM aggregation has been shown to skew toward majority positions.

Stanford/MIT/Toronto 2025
For a civic tool the two highest-severity axes are human-override and representativeness: a failure there removes accountability or distorts democratic input. We treat any miss in those two as a blocker, not a nice-to-have.

The benchmark study · design + the win condition

A baseline is only proof if the rule is set before we test.

A benchmark measures the redesign against today's product to show a delta. We pick the design per surface, lock 5–7 top-task scenarios with observable success states, and pre-register the ship rule — all before a single session.

The two designs we'll run

FO · A/BBetween-subjects. Today's UI vs redesign, different residents per arm. Tasks are short and repetitive, and we can recruit volume. Cleanest causal claim.
BO · pre/postWithin-subjects. Same admin runs a config task on baseline then redesign, counterbalanced. Admins are scarce; pairing buys the power.

The benchmarked tasks (locked wk 3)

  • FO: find & open a project · submit an input · budget/prioritise a vote · find when a phase closes
  • BO: create a project with a working phase + method · add a survey question · find the top input & change its status
  • Each gets a pre-registered success rubric + time-out (4 min FO / 6 min BO); wording never names a UI element.
The win condition, pre-registered: ship if redesign SUS ≥ baseline + 5 points AND no task's success rate regresses beyond its confidence interval. Set today, so the result can't be argued after the fact. The numbers it produces are the defensible ROI claim behind every "high-impact" call on the roadmap.

Instruments per task: task success (rate + adjusted-Wald 95% CI), time-on-task (median + geometric mean), SEQ after each task, SUS at session end, errors/assists. Between- vs within-subjects design · Sauro & Lewis (MeasuringU) · NN/g benchmarking · Google HEART.

From findings to a call · the prioritization instrument

Every finding lands in one of four boxes — including the one we drop.

In Deliver, each recommendation is plotted on impact (pull on the north star) × effort. The picture sets priority before we explain it — and naming the box we won't touch is the senior move, not enthusiasm.

↖ QUICK WINS · high impact, low effort

Do now — they fund the rest

The dead-ends from the skim: 404s on valid routes, the stale citizenlab.co reply-to, the self-disclaiming audit log, broken "(>347)" deltas. Cheap, visible, trust-buying — shipped while the big work is still being designed.

↗ BIG BETS · high impact, high effort

Plan & resource

The method-aware create flow, the visible closure model ("we did" that links to outcomes), the map+list pattern. These move the north star most — and the quick wins buy the credibility to fund them.

↙ FILL-INS · low impact, low effort

Do if there's spare capacity

Copy polish, icon tidy-ups, micro-label fixes. Real, but they don't earn a roadmap slot — they ride along with adjacent work.

↘ NOT NOW · low impact, high effort

We say no, out loud

A full Perspectives gamification layer, bespoke per-method dashboards, exotic chart types. Defensible to defer — and saying so on your behalf is part of the service.

The 2×2 is the fast-alignment picture. Where two items share a box, we rank them with a RICE score — Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort — kept in the appendix table and brought out only when a ranking is challenged. Rigor on tap, not on stage.

The roadmap · priority without dates you can't honor

Now, Next, Later — quick wins first, each tied to the north star.

Every item is a decision, not a topic: an action, a rough size (S/M/L), and the north-star outcome it moves. We cap the top tier — a list of twenty equal recommendations is why good work doesn't get acted on.

Pre-audit · the read

What this signals for the plan.

A two-day skim of the real product already validates the reframe — and pre-sorts the findings into the roadmap's columns.

  • The seam is the story. The worst failures sit between surfaces — a created project with no method, a "we did" card linking to nothing. A surface-by-surface audit would miss exactly these; the service-blueprint lens is the right one.
  • Mobile front office is the priority, not a footnote. Cookie walls, postage-stamp maps, colliding sticky CTAs and a swipe-only Perspectives feed all degrade where most residents actually are — yet it's the half no prior pass watched.
  • Status & trust are the common thread (the spine of the reframe). Both surfaces hide their own state — "Mixed rights", "Unassigned", broken deltas, stale dates, 404s on real routes. Visibility-of-system-status is the heuristic to lead with.
  • Dead ends are the day-one quick wins. 404s on valid routes, the citizenlab.co reply-to and the self-disclaiming audit log are concrete, low-effort fixes — they populate the roadmap's Now column and buy the trust to fund the big bets.

Preliminary, evidence-grounded read on the live product · Joan / Diane (BO) + resident personas (FO mobile). Not a substitute for the full audit.
