The opportunity

Three prior passes mapped the back-office build flow in detail. None has watched a resident try to discover, understand, and finish a contribution — or asked whether the back office and front office add up to one coherent service.

  • The product is one loop, not two backlogs. A flawless project residents can't use is a failed project; a low completion rate becomes thin analysis.
  • The friction lives in the seams between the two surfaces — exactly what surface-by-surface audits miss.
  • The timing forces it now — cost, competition and compliance are converging (next slide).

Method → the question it answers

We pick the method for the question — and triangulate.

QuestionMethodWhy
Does it break basic usability?Heuristic + agentic auditFast, evidence-tagged; the method proven in May.
Can users do the core jobs?JTBD scripts + 5-user tests~3 residents per group; moderated for the murky BO flows.
Is the structure findable?Tree test + first-clickFirst click ~doubles task success; cheap to run.
Where & how often is friction?Analytics funnel + session replayQuantifies the drop-offs the audit should target.
Which tasks even matter?Top-tasks analysisScopes the audit to the vital few, not the trivial many.
Did we actually improve?Benchmark (baseline → re-measure)Same tasks, before and after — the credible ROI proof.

Triangulate qual + quant: analytics shows what, testing shows why. NN/g · Optimal Workshop · McGovern · MeasuringU.

Who we audit for

Both sides of the loop — five people.

Back office · staff

Joan · high savviness

Weekly user, 15+ projects, own shortcuts. Benchmark is speed; skips help text.

Diane · low savviness

Small municipality, minimal training. Hesitates, re-reads, relies on the UI to say what's next.

Front office · residents

The rare participant

Low digital confidence. First 30 seconds decide everything; jargon or a login wall loses them.

The engaged resident

Wants depth and outcomes. Returns only if the last loop visibly closed.

The skeptic

Doubts it's worth it. Needs trust and impact obvious up front.

We'll run a scoped WCAG 2.2 AA check (automated + keyboard + screen-reader spot-checks) on representative templates — forms, a multi-step flow, a map, a content page — focusing on the criteria that bite: target size, dragging alternatives, accessible authentication, focus not obscured. Dimension 2 · AI we can trust

The AI bet only pays off if residents trust it.

Perspectives, AI Sensemaking, Common Ground and FormSync are central to the roadmap. We'll audit them against the established AI-UX frameworks on three axes:

Explainability

Show sources and calibrated confidence — so staff and residents can understand and challenge a result.

Google PAIR · MS HAX

Human oversight

Every AI summary, cluster and tag must be editable and overridable in place — legally required for high-risk public use.

EU AI Act Art. 14

Representativeness

AI must not silence minority voices — a risk now measured at 34× random in LLM opinion-aggregation. The whole sensemaking promise rests here.

Stanford/MIT/Toronto 2025

Dimension 3 · benchmark, don't guess

Compare against the best — for what they're best at.

DELIBERATION

vs Polis

One-tap agree/disagree + a live consensus map. Lowest-friction contribution and richest deliberation at once — our biggest experience gap.

BACK OFFICE

vs EngagementHQ

Project templating / cloning and layered site→project→tool reporting. Worth matching for staff efficiency.

CLOSING THE LOOP

vs Decidim

Follow a proposal through visible implementation stages — closure as a data model, not a marketing page. The #1 trust failure sector-wide.

compdemocracy.org · granicus.com · docs.decidim.org. Turnout reality: Decide Madrid reached ~90k active of a 3.2M city — the front-office experience is the participation rate.

Civic & business

Launch ↓Time-to-launch and support-ticket deflection (Intercom).
CompleteFO completion + return rate — repeat participation.
WhoRepresentativeness vs population — not raw volume.

Baseline → redesign → re-measure the same tasks = the defensible ROI claim. MeasuringU / Sauro · Google HEART. Benchmarks are industry averages, to be re-based on our own data.
