Why now

Three risks the experience is creating today.

Our Q1 2026 Intercom analysis surfaced a consistent pattern: a meaningful share of client friction and support load traces back to the core flows — users hitting basic orientation questions mid-task, while actively building or running a consultation.

🚀

Activation & time-to-value

Setup complexity slows "I need a consultation" → "I'm live." Slower activation, weaker always-on engagement.

💬

Support cost

Repeat Intercom load tied to avoidable UX confusion — overhead that a clear interface should remove, not absorb.

📈

Growth ceiling

Complexity becomes a blocker in deals and a drag on retention as we scale across 500+ governments.

We've already audited one half — well.

Two prior passes mapped the back-office project-creation flow and proved a repeatable method. This restart widens the lens; it doesn't discard that work.

The blind spot

Every pass so far stopped at the back office.

Nobody has audited the front office — the resident's actual participation experience. That's the public, branded half of the product, and it's the half that decides whether the back-office work even matters.

  • A perfectly configured project that residents can't discover, understand, or finish is still a failed project.
  • The 24 findings tell us how staff build a consultation — nothing about how residents experience it.
  • The two surfaces aren't separate products. They're one loop, and the friction lives in the handoffs between them.

The reframe

Audit the whole participation loop.

Front office residents

1 Discover a project
2 Understand & trust it
3 Participate (idea · vote · survey)
4 See the loop close
→
input flows
to staff
←
outcome flows
to residents

Back office staff

A Set up the project
B Configure the method
C Moderate & manage
D Make sense & report
Friction compounds at the seams. Unclear access-rights config (finding F12) becomes an unexpected login wall for a resident. A confusing survey builder becomes a low completion rate becomes thin analysis. Auditing the halves separately misses exactly these joins.

What we're answering

Four questions — across both surfaces.

1
Who are our users?
Staff (dedicated officers, high & low savviness) and residents (low-literacy, engaged, skeptical first-timer). Which segment do we optimise for?
2
Where does the loop break?
A friction inventory across every BO and FO stage, each finding tagged to a usability principle and backed by evidence.
3
Which breaks matter most?
Severity (0–4) × frequency × business impact — activation, support cost, participation, trust. Mapped on an impact/effort 2×2.
4
What improves when we fix them?
A target metric per surface, so every recommendation lands on a number this room already owns.

Who we audit for

Two sides of the loop, five people.

Back office · staff

Joan — high savviness

Uses GoVocal weekly, 15+ projects, has her own shortcuts. Benchmark is speed; rarely reads help.

Diane — low savviness

Small municipality, minimal training. Hesitates, re-reads labels, relies on the UI to tell her what's next.

Front office · residents

The rare participant

Low digital confidence. First 30 seconds decide everything; jargon or a login wall loses them.

The engaged resident

Wants depth and to see outcomes. Returns only if the last loop visibly closed.

The skeptic

Doubts it's worth it. Won't participate unless trust and impact are obvious up front.

Coverage

The loop, audited stage by stage.

Mapped to GoVocal's spine — Folder → Project → Phase → method. We go end-to-end on 2–3 representative methods (ideation, survey, voting), not all eight exhaustively.

Front office

  • Discover — homepage, project list, search, "follow by interest"
  • Understand — project page, phases, what's being asked & why
  • Participate — input form, voting, survey, map, mobile web
  • Closure — statuses, official feedback, "you said / we did"

Back office

  • Set up — entry point, identity, access rights, timeline
  • Configure — method choice, form / input / voting builders
  • Moderate — Input Manager, statuses, internal collaboration
  • Make sense — Insights, AI analysis, Report Builder, close loop

What "good" looks like

Every fix lands on a metric.

Time‑to‑launchBack office — faster activation, fewer setup steps to "live."
Ticket ↓Support deflection — Intercom volume from the audited flows.
Task successStaff complete configuration without error or backtracking.
CompletionFront office — residents who start a contribution and finish it.
Return rateRepeat participation — driven by visibly closing the loop.
WhoRepresentativeness vs population — a high count from a narrow slice is a failure, not a win.
The sharpest civic storyline: closing the loop → public trust → repeat participation. It's also the metric most often gamed by raw volume — so we always segment by who actually showed up.

Scope guardrails

What's in — and what protects the timeline.

In scope

  • The whole loop — both surfaces, the handoffs between them
  • 2–3 methods end-to-end (ideation, survey, voting)
  • FO mobile web — residents are mobile-first
  • A WCAG 2.2 AA check (flag, don't certify)

Out of scope

  • Full redesign execution — this is a vision + priorities, not production design
  • Full accessibility certification
  • Native mobile app internals
  • Exhaustive coverage of all 8 methods
